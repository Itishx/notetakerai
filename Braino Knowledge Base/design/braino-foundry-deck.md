# The Braino Foundry deck

**Type:** Design decision
**Platform:** Braino website
**Status:** MVP
**Released:** August 16, 2026

## The simple version

`/deck` is the Braino Foundry pitch, rebuilt as a page instead of a PDF. Thirty six slides, Apple product-page design language, and the three things the original PDF left out: who Braino already is, the fact that there are two kinds of him, and the business.

`/pitch` is the same page under a friendlier URL to say out loud.

## Why it exists

The original `braino-foundry-deck.pdf` was twelve slides and it made one argument well: building is easy now, getting seen is the hard part, and Braino Foundry is two engines pointed at exactly that. Braino Code builds, Distra distributes.

What it never explained is everything that makes Braino fundable in the first place. It opened on a coding agent, so a reader met a tool. There was no character, no Braino OS, no memory, no emotional engine, and no browser product. Someone reading it could not tell why this particular team would win, or what the free product had to do with the paid one.

The deck also priced itself without saying what a person is buying. Free, credits, upgrade is a billing model, not a product. And it had no business section at all: no market, no comparables, no route to market. An investor cannot size an opportunity from a features list.

## How to use it

1. Open `/deck` or `/pitch`.
2. Scroll, or use the arrow keys, page keys or space. Each slide snaps to the viewport.
3. The rail on the right is grouped by act, so the shape of the deck is visible from any slide. Click a dot to jump.
4. Home and End go to the cover and the close.
5. Print gives one slide per page.

## What the deck says

The order is the argument:

1. **The shift.** Building is easy now, getting seen is the whole game, and the solo founder pays for both.
2. **Who Braino already is.** The half the PDF skipped: he is a character with a face, a voice, a memory and an opinion, and every surface runs the same Braino OS.
3. **Two kinds of Braino.** Personal Braino is free. Founder Braino is the paid co-founder. Same character, two jobs.
4. **The solution.** One co-founder, two engines: Braino Code and Distra.
5. **How it ties together.** The loop that a stack of separate tools cannot do.
6. **Why us, why now.** The machinery is built, and it took three months.
7. **The business.** Market, the shape of the customer, comparables, and go to market.
8. **Money**, then the close.

### The two Brainos

This is the new spine of the deck and the part that did not exist before.

**Personal Braino is free, on purpose.** Free in the browser, free to simply be on your screen, with the full personality and the full memory. Charging for a friend on your screen is a bad business and a worse product. The only paid part is the Mac companion, expected around five to ten dollars a month, and that number is explicitly not final.

**Founder Braino is the one you hire.** The co-founder on macOS, carrying Braino Code and Distra, priced on usage.

The commercial argument is the sequence: Personal Braino is how a founder meets him, Founder Braino is what they buy once they trust him. The deck states that out loud rather than leaving it implied.

### The business act

Four slides, because a deck that ends at pricing has answered the wrong question.

**The market.** TAM, SAM and SOM as three stepped bars. TAM is $45B: the 2026 software spend on building it (AI code assistants, $10.3B to $16.1B depending on whose definition) plus the spend on being seen (social media management, $33.5B to $36.4B). SAM is $3B, bottom-up: roughly five million US solopreneurs already earning six figures, at about $600 a year. SOM is $15M ARR, being 25,000 paying founders inside three years, about half a percent of the SAM.

The bars are illustrative, not to scale. A real-area TAM/SOM drawing makes the SOM invisible and the slide useless. The numbers carry the argument; the bars only carry the direction.

**Who is starting companies now.** 36.3% of new startups in the first half of 2025 had a single founder, up from 23.7% in 2019. 29.8 million solo business owners in the US alone, turning over about $1.7 trillion. 73% of solopreneurs already use AI. The market does not have to be created, only aimed at.

**Comparables.** Lovable at $500M ARR and a $13.2B valuation with 146 people. Cursor at roughly $4B ARR by mid 2026, with a $60B acquisition agreed in June. Both of them sell building. Neither of them sells being seen. That is the whole wedge, on one slide.

**Go to market.** Braino distributes Braino. Distra's first customer is the company itself, the character is the marketing rather than the thing marketing pays for, free is the funnel, and acquisition is content rather than ads.

Then two money slides: the model (three revenue lines, only one of them a subscription, revenue scaling with work done rather than seats sold) and what funding buys (inference headroom, the first hires, distribution that compounds).

### Three months, not eighteen

The pace slide says three months, because that is the real number. Braino did not exist in May 2026, and the character, voice, memory, Mac app, browser extension and distribution engine were all built between then and now. The commit count on that slide is the real count since the pivot.

### The honest ledger

One slide splits the product into Built, Building and Next, and says plainly that the site is a waitlist today. Braino Code and Distra are in the Building column, not the Built one. A deck that overstates shipping status is the one thing worse than a slow roadmap.

## The static solar system

The ecosystem slide carries a static version of the Braino solar system: Braino OS at the centre with his eyes on, and Braino Browser, the Mac companion, Braino Foundry, BrainoHERA and BrainoStudio around it.

Static is the decision, not a limitation. The animated system on the product pages sells presence; this one has to be read correctly in about four seconds while somebody is talking over it. So nothing orbits, every label is always visible, and colour carries the commercial split: coral is the free personal family, blue is the paid co-founder.

Below 820px the diagram is replaced by the same information as a list, because shrinking it would have made the labels unreadable rather than smaller.

The loop diagram on the "how it ties together" slide works the same way: four stations on a dashed ring with Braino OS in the middle, static, with a list fallback on phones.

## The three clarity slides

Added 17 August 2026, after a read-through where the deck argued well but never
stated its own thesis plainly and never answered the question every investor
asks out loud.

### Slide 01, before any act: what the market sells you versus what we sell you

A four row table, read across rather than down: building the product, getting it
seen, how your tools fit, and what it knows about you. Row aligned on purpose,
because the contrast only lands when the same job is seen in two different
worlds. It closes on the sentence the whole company rests on, which is that
every company in this market sells you the building and nobody sells you the
being seen.

It sits second, immediately after the cover, so a reader can state the thesis
before act one has even opened.

### Where is the human in the loop

The question most AI decks dodge. The answer has a shape, so the slide draws it:
a four step chain colour coded by whose hands are on each step, You, then Braino,
then You again, then public. The founder holds both ends. Underneath it, three
columns name the split without hedging: what is always yours, what is always his,
and the hard gates where he stops and asks, which are money, anything published,
messaging a human, deleting anything, and changing a price or a promise.

The line that carries it: he decides how, you decide whether.

### The one page summary

Last slide before the close, so whoever is in the room can photograph one thing
and still have the argument a week later. Five numbered bands down a hairline
spine: the shift, the mind, two Brainos, two engines, the money. The joins sit
between each pair rather than beneath it, with a one way arrow for trust first
hire later and a two way arrow for the signal loop, because a label under a row
of two boxes reads as an arrow down to the next band.

Built in markup rather than SVG, unlike the solar system and the loop. Those are
fixed drawings with short labels; this one carries real sentences, and SVG text
does not wrap. Grid bands wrap, reflow on a phone, and stay selectable, which
matters for the slide someone actually copies out.

## Honest limitations

The deck is positioning and a web page. It is not a fundraise, a data room, or a commitment to the pricing on the money slide, which is explicitly marked as not final.

Braino Code and Distra are named as engines and shown in the Building column. Neither is a shipped product today, and the deck should not be edited to imply otherwise while that is true.

The commit figure on the pace slide is a point-in-time number and will drift. It says "1,100+" for that reason, but it still needs a look before the deck is shown to anybody who counts.

The market slide is the weakest part of the deck by construction, because every market slide is. TAM is a range of analyst estimates for two adjacent categories, not a measured number, and the SAM and SOM are ours. The comparable figures move fast: Cursor went from $2B to roughly $4B ARR in four months, so those numbers need re-checking before any serious conversation.

There is no ask in the deck. Round size, terms and use of proceeds are deliberately absent and the slide says so. That is a decision, not an omission, but it does mean the deck cannot be sent cold to an investor and do the whole job on its own.

## Product or design decisions

**Light the whole way, with two near-black bookends.** A dark slab in the middle of a light page reads as a mistake. A dark cover and a dark close read as a cover and a close.

**One accent per act.** Coral for the problem, violet for who Braino is, blue for the co-founder and Braino Code, green for Distra, amber for money and both diagrams. The accent drives the kicker, the headline highlight, the rail dot and the progress line together, so the act is visible without a label.

**Act dividers instead of a table of contents.** Five numbered dividers carry the shape of the argument. They are not numbered as slides, and the counter shows a dash on them rather than a fake number.

**Every slide fits one viewport.** A deck slide that needs scrolling is two slides pretending to be one. Two modifiers do that work rather than hand-tuning each slide: `is-dense` tightens type on the heavy slides, and `has-diagram` sizes the drawing off viewport height, so the picture gives way before the words do. Verified at 1440x900 and 1280x720.

**The character is introduced before the pitch.** The original deck opened on a coding agent. This one spends an act on who Braino is first, because the product argument only works if the reader already believes there is someone there.

**The comparables slide names the gap rather than the competition.** Lovable and Cursor are not framed as rivals to beat. They are framed as proof that the build half of the thesis is already a funded, fast-growing category, which makes the unclaimed half the interesting part. A competition slide argues about features; this one argues about what is missing.

**Sources are on the slide, not in an appendix.** Anyone who has sat through a market slide knows the first question is where the number came from. Answering it before it is asked costs a line of 12px grey type and buys the rest of the section.

## What comes next

Keep the pace slide's commit number current, move Braino Code and Distra out of the Building column only when they genuinely ship, and settle the Personal Braino Mac companion price before the deck is used in a real conversation about money.

Re-check the comparable ARR and valuation figures before every serious showing; they were current in August 2026 and that category moves in months. Replace the SOM with real conversion data the moment there is any, because a bottom-up guess stops being acceptable the day the first hundred people pay. And decide whether the ask belongs in the deck or stays in the conversation.
